Where service businesses lose revenue after the quote

Owners obsess over getting more leads. But for most service businesses, the bigger leak is on the other side of the quote — in the follow-up that never happens.

Every service business — landscaping, HVAC, roofing, cleaning, plumbing, electrical — spends real money to generate a lead and produce an estimate. By the time a quote goes out, you've already paid for the marketing, the phone call, the site visit, and the time to price it. That estimate is the most expensive asset in your pipeline. And it's the one most likely to be abandoned.

Here's where the money quietly leaks out after the quote.

Leak 1: The estimate that's never followed up

The customer didn't say no. They said "let me think about it" — and then life happened on both sides. Without a scheduled follow-up, that quote simply ages out. It's the single most common leak, and it has nothing to do with price.

Leak 2: The reply that gets missed

The customer did respond — a text, an email, a voicemail — but it landed during a busy stretch and no one circled back fast enough. Speed matters: the business that responds first usually wins the job. A reply with no owner is a sale with no closer.

Leak 3: The pipeline no one can see

When quotes live in separate inboxes, texts, and heads, no one can answer a simple question: which estimates are still open, and what's the next action on each? What you can't see, you can't work. Invisible pipeline equals forgotten pipeline.

Leak 4: No one is accountable

"Someone will follow up" is not accountability — it's a hope shared by a team that each assumes another person has it. Real accountability means every estimate has a name and a next action, and the exceptions surface to one responsible person instead of disappearing.

Plugging the leaks

None of these leaks are fixed by working harder or buying another app. They're fixed by a system that makes every estimate visible, follows up on a schedule, stops and routes automatically, and flags what needs a human. That's the mechanism behind the PeakReach Revenue Recovery System — and the accountability discipline of The PeakReach Standard™.

It was proven first inside a real landscaping company, but the leaks are universal to service businesses. If you send estimates, you have a follow-up leak — the only question is how big it is.
