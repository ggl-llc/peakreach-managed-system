CRM for contractors: the bids you wrote are the revenue you're losing

Contractors don't lose jobs on price as often as they think. They lose them in the silence after the proposal — while the client hears from someone else. PeakReach installs and operates the system that follows up on every bid, so the CRM stops being a filing cabinet and starts being a process.

Search "contractor CRM" and you'll find tools built for estimating, scheduling, change orders, and invoicing. Most of them are fine. Most of them also share one weakness: the proposal goes out, the tool records it, and then nothing happens unless a person remembers. On a job site, nobody remembers.

For remodelers, general contractors, specialty trades, and commercial service contractors, three things decide whether a CRM pays for itself.

1. Every open bid, visible with an owner

You should be able to see, on one screen, every proposal that's out — how long it's been out, who owns it, and what the next touch is. Not a spreadsheet the estimator keeps. A pipeline the office can read without asking.

2. Follow-up that runs on a schedule and stops on a reply

Approved email and text touches go out at day 2, day 5, day 10 — without anyone typing them — and stop the moment the client answers, books, or opts out. Consistency is what wins the comparison the client is running in their head.

3. Exceptions that land on a person

A reply, a question about scope, a proposal aging past two weeks: those need judgment. A working system flags them to a named person instead of letting them sit unread. That is what accountability looks like when it's structural rather than personal.

Why contractors don't get this from software alone

Because software waits to be operated. Someone has to configure the sequences, watch the exception queue, fix the integration when the estimating tool changes its export, and report what actually happened last week. In a contracting company that person is usually the owner — which is exactly the dependency PeakReach exists to remove.

PeakReach is not a CRM company. We install and operate the Revenue Recovery System around the tools you already bid from, under The PeakReach Standard™: configure, integrate, QA, launch, stabilize — then run it and report weekly. You keep your data and client-owned accounts; we keep the system running.
